# Teach with a worked example

State the small task and its expected result. Supply the prerequisites needed to
run or follow it. Use one example throughout, adding a concept when the next step
needs it. Explain each step's purpose beside the step.

Choose a task that makes the target relationship visible. Prefer a reduced case
from the reader's material when it fits. Keep the conditions responsible for its
behavior and identify a constructed case as hypothetical. Explain both the
action and the reason it works under those conditions.

Show intermediate values where a learner could lose the causal thread. Include
a check that distinguishes success from a plausible failure. Explain how changing
one input changes the result. Adapt the level of detail to the reader and retain
version or environment requirements that affect reproducibility.

When the reader needs to apply the idea elsewhere, connect the worked steps to
the general relationship. Use a brief changed case to show which details matter.
For guided practice, let the reader predict a step if they want to; for a written
walkthrough, show and explain the outcome. Continue past established knowledge.
