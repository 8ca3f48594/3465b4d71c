# Mental models and usability

## Scope

Explain user expectations, interface behavior, and usability mismatches.

## Prerequisites and distinctions

Identify the user's prediction and the system's actual behavior. Separate an observed interaction from a guess about motivation.

## Teaching sequence

Describe one action, the expected outcome, the actual outcome, and the evidence for the mismatch. Suggest a change that can be tested.

## Common misconceptions

The designer's understanding may differ from the user's. A confusing action does not establish that the user is careless.

## Example 1

Suppose a user expects closing a settings panel to save changes, but the product requires a Save action. The explanation should identify that mismatch and show the visible confirmation the product actually provides.

## Example 2

If a label says Archive while users expect deletion, observe what users predict before changing the label. The problem may concern terminology, behavior, or both; the label alone does not establish their understanding.

## Analogies and limits

A map represents a person's working picture of a system. It can omit details or contain errors; it does not reveal the person's entire reasoning process.
