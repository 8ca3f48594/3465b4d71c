# Teamwork and decision-making

## Scope

Explain collaboration, speaking up, decision evidence, and team learning.

## Prerequisites and distinctions

Identify the decision, roles, and observed behavior. Separate a team-level association from a claim about an individual.

## Teaching sequence

Start with the concrete interaction. Explain what information was shared or withheld and what decision it affected. Keep causal claims within the evidence.

## Common misconceptions

Psychological safety does not mean every proposal is accepted. An observational association does not prove a particular management action will cause an improvement.

## Example 1

Edmondson's study found an association between team psychological safety and learning behavior in the teams studied. That finding supports examining whether people can raise concerns, but does not establish a guaranteed intervention for every software team.

## Example 2

A review comment can identify a failure case and invite evidence about it. The team can then check the case against the code. This proposed practice makes disagreement inspectable without treating disagreement itself as a defect.

## Analogies and limits

Use the actual review conversation and decision record. A team-as-machine analogy leaves out interpretation, incentives, and interpersonal risk.
