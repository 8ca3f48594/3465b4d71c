# Attention and cognitive load

## Scope

Explain how task demands and presentation can affect learning or comprehension.

## Prerequisites and distinctions

Identify what must be held in mind and what knowledge the reader already has. Separate task difficulty from unnecessary presentation demands.

## Teaching sequence

Use one concrete task. Show the information needed at each step and reduce avoidable cross-references. Present adaptations as teaching choices.

## Common misconceptions

Conciseness alone does not reduce difficulty. Removing a needed intermediate step can increase the reader's work.

## Example 1

A beginner reading an equation may need the variable definitions beside it. Placing them nearby is a teaching choice intended to reduce the need to remember definitions while following the calculation.

## Example 2

A worked example can expose intermediate steps before asking a learner to solve a similar problem. Sweller's work connects problem-solving demands with learning; it does not establish one best format for every level of expertise.

## Analogies and limits

A limited workspace can illustrate competing demands, but it is not a fixed measurement of a person's memory capacity.
