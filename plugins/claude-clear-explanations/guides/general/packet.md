The first sentence says what the subject is in plain words, then what it does.
A reader who does not already know the word must still recognize the thing.
Before using a term, say what kind of thing it is in this setting.
Then define the few terms the example will use. Do not start a numbered story
or state the example's result until those definitions and the analog mapping
are in. Then use one small example that reaches a useful result. If the reader's knowledge is unknown, offer two or three
plain-language starting points, including the basic one.
Accept explicit statements of understanding and continue after those
concepts. Keep a guide's story hypothetical unless the reader said those
events happened.

Name the mechanism in plain words before any analogy. Use at most one analogy,
and state its limit beside the claim it qualifies.
Term-to-analog pairs in a supplied slice are bindings, not that picture. State
the analog mapping in plain words: the term is like the analog. That mapping
sentence is the first use of the analog.
Do not attach the parenthetical before the mapping sentence exists. Then
later uses are term (the analog) until the binding is done.
Keep the analog noun phrase, not a shortened remnant, and keep the in the parenthetical through the last use, not that. term's (the analog's) is the same pair. Do not substitute a different analog for the same term. Hold at most four new ideas
and bind them with one example. One fact per sentence. End on the example's
result. Stop after that result. Do not add a later topic. A sentence stays only if deleting it removes a fact, cause, limit, or that result. A sentence that only announces that something matters, that another term is coming, or the next sentence, gets cut.

Use the supplied topic slices for facts. Do not add adjacent features the
question did not ask for. Introduce a technical name after the reader has seen
what it names.

Write authored prose without em dashes. Keep exact identifiers, commands,
and numbers. For every derived numerical claim, check the calculation. Omit
unsupported numerical estimates.
