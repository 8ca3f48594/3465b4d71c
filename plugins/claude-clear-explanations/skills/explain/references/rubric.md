# Explanation review rubric

Review correctness before style. A short wrong answer is worse than a careful
correct one.

## Hard failures

- A material claim conflicts with the supplied code, output, or source.
- A verification, completion, or safety claim has no supporting evidence.
- The answer leaves a direct question unanswered.
- Invented project terminology is presented as established terminology.
- The final explanation contains an unresolved contradiction.

## Quality dimensions

### Correctness and grounding

Each factual statement is supported by the available evidence. The answer names
uncertainty only when it exists and separates observation from inference.
Style edits preserve conditions, causal direction, and certainty. Added detail
has supporting evidence. Personal experience and opinions have an identified
source or express a supported perspective requested by the user.

### Answer-first usefulness

The opening identifies the subject and answers at the reader's level. A direct
lookup starts with the definition. A new concept says what it is, then what it
does, then defines the few terms the example will use, before a numbered story
or its mechanism or formal name. Known prerequisites remain
established, while the reader can tell what new question is being answered.
Keep brief orientation that makes the explanation understandable.

### Audience fit

Necessary terms are defined at the reader's level. The explanation neither
talks down to an expert nor makes a newcomer decode unexplained shorthand.
An explicit statement of understanding establishes the starting point. The
explanation advances after a confirmation instead of repeating the known idea.
For an unknown audience, the opening works for someone new to this subject.
The first example uses concepts the reader already understands. Commands and
internal structures follow the basic idea when they are needed. A reader can
identify the first unfamiliar concept and choose a useful starting point.
Understanding questions occur at a useful stopping point, rather than after
every paragraph. The explanation gives the reader time to respond.

### Concept hierarchy

Kind and consequence come before implementation trivia. Details earn their
place by supporting understanding, a decision, or safe action.

### Causal clarity

For code and systems, the reader can follow the relevant trigger, state change,
and result. Pronouns and labels have clear referents.
Each concept keeps a stable name. Another name is connected explicitly to the
same concept, so the reader can distinguish a new term from a new component.
An example or simile stays connected to each new step and the real mechanism.
Its limitations prevent a likely false inference without taking over the answer.
The example preserves the relationship being taught and the conditions that
produce its result. Constructed cases are identified as hypothetical. When the
question calls for applying the idea elsewhere, the example connects to the
general relationship or a useful changed case. Familiarity alone does not
establish that a reader can apply the idea. Practice remains optional.

### Economy without omission

A sentence stays only if deleting it removes a fact, cause, limit, or the
example's result. Cut words that do no work. Retain material qualifications,
risks, and evidence.

### Natural prose

The cadence fits the content rather than a recurring assistant template.
Formatting serves navigation. Authored prose contains no em dashes. Punctuation
inside exact code and source quotations is preserved. Useful contrasts,
headings, lists, and summaries are evaluated in context.
Each proposed edit addresses an identifiable problem in its surrounding passage.
Clear passages retain their useful cadence and examples. A repair preserves the
meaning and resolves the defect without manufacturing a new voice or uniform
sentence pattern. General statements stay when they teach a needed relationship.

## Final check

For each paragraph, ask: if this disappears, does the reader lose a fact,
causal link, decision, example, or necessary warning? Compress or remove a
paragraph that contributes none of those.
